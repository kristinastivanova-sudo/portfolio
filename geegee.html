The challenge

GeeGee is a social mobile app for creating and playing MicroSports: short, fun physical challenges that people invent, perform and share with friends. The vision was to make sports more social, playful and Gen Z-friendly.

When I joined, the team lacked clarity on its users, its positioning and its content direction. Once the app was live, the job became finding those users at a cost a small startup could sustain, across three very different markets.

What I did

From research to a growing audience

01

Research & personas

Surveyed ~50 users and ran 1:1 interviews, then defined three personas: a teen creator, a student athlete and a young professional who wants to stay active.

02

Positioning & messaging

“Create and challenge your world with MicroSports”, with separate copy for creators, players, spectators and partners.

03

Core loop & onboarding

Mapped the watch → attempt → play → create loop and wrote onboarding, headline and CTA copy for each persona.

04

Paid acquisition

Ran install campaigns in NZ, AU and the UK, testing channels, sports (rugby vs soccer), platforms and audiences week by week.

Monthly active users

Growth sped up once paid campaigns found the right channels: about +45% a month in July and August, compared with +21% to +33% before.

Paid acquisition

More installs for less: what changed in July

01
Awareness didn't convert
In May, campaigns reached 1.4M impressions, mostly awareness, but brought 3 installs: NZ$498 each. Budget moved to install-optimised campaigns.
02
Retargeting the warm audience
Retargeting people who had already engaged brought installs for NZ$5.38 each on Meta in July, versus NZ$49 for cold NZ audiences in June.
03
Going where Gen Z is
TikTok and Snapchat launched in July and drove 1,080 installs in two months, 92% of paid installs in that period.
