Lowering the cost per purchase for a beauty brand on Google and Meta

Ten months running Google and Meta Ads for a lip-care brand, with ad and Google Analytics data blended into one view so budget followed what actually sold.

Role
Google & Meta Ads management
Timeline
10 months
Channels
Google Ads · Meta Ads
Reporting
Google Analytics + ad platforms, blended
Results

More purchases for less

−38%
Meta cost per purchase, Jan–Feb vs May–Jul 2023
822

~2×
Purchases in promo weeks (Valentine’s, Easter) vs a normal week
−31%
Google Ads cost per click, Sep 2022 to Jan 2023
From May, weekly Meta spend was roughly halved while purchases held steady or grew.

The challenge

Luxe Lips sells lip-care products online. Sales came in through direct visits, Instagram, organic search, email and paid ads, and each platform reported its own version of the truth.

The job was to grow purchases from Google and Meta without the cost per purchase running away, and to show clearly which spend was paying off.

What I did

Budget that follows sales

01

One view of every channel

Blended Google Analytics, Google Ads and Meta data by month and week, so paid results sat next to direct, organic and email.

02

Seasonal offers

Planned ads around promos such as Valentine’s Special, Kissable Deal, Easter Bundle and Winter Glow. Promo weeks roughly doubled purchases.

03

Spend where it converts

Cut weekly Meta spend from around 2.5K to 1.1–1.3K once the data showed where purchases came from, and cost per purchase dropped 38%.

04

Lead capture

Added a lead campaign from late May, so the brand kept building a list between purchase-focused campaigns.
